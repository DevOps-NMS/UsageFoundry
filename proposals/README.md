# Proposals

Design proposals that are not yet decisions. A directory here surveys one
question, gives every candidate its strongest honest case, and ends with a
single recommendation and the fact that would overturn it. Nothing in here
describes the code as it stands — `docs/agent/` is where the invariants that
have already been decided live, and `docs/` proper is what an operator reads.
A proposal is promoted by implementing it and moving its reasoning into those
two places; until then, treat every file here as an argument rather than a
record.

Every factual claim carries a `path/file.ts:42` reference or the command whose
output it quotes. A claim that could not be verified says "assumed" in the
sentence that makes it.

| [ContextControl](ContextControl/README.md) | What does a run carry from one work cycle to the next, what does carrying it cost, and can this app make it carry less? | Open; one recommendation, and it is **against building any of the thirteen mechanisms** — build the per-cycle composition readout, now including the compaction boundary, plus the two repairs the survey found while looking for something else. 82% of the bill is carried context, but at a tenth of the input rate, so that is the prompt cache's discount rather than waste; the one identified waste is 6.1% of the bill and its cause sits in the CLI's own system prompt. **Revised 2026-08-22** against context research in the operator's vault: the survey scored on cost, every published measurement since prices context compression as a *correctness* event, so an eleventh criterion at weight 4 re-scores the field — the recommendation holds and its lead widens from 3 points to 9, the runner-up changes from delegation to moving the volatile prefix, and app-driven compaction falls to last with three independent measurements landing on its exact mechanism. Two of the survey's own observations were refuted by probe and both in its favour: the CLI's compaction leaves a full marker in the transcript (twenty of them on this install, and nothing reads it), and a compaction **does** survive `--resume`. The provider's own `context_management` block is Option M and is rejected on **reachability** — five converging probes say no argv this app emits and no `--settings` key reaches it at CLI 2.1.226. Unverified: that a fresh conversation per cycle finishes the same task in the same number of work cycles, which is the single-digit-dollar experiment that would overturn it; whether a re-injected rule still *binds* after a compaction as opposed to merely being present, which nobody anywhere has measured and which sets the new criterion's weight; and whether the API's prefix match ends at a cache breakpoint, which is the difference between $1.44 and $5.02 a week for the one flag that reaches the mechanism |
| [GapRegister](GapRegister/README.md) | Where are this app's gaps — across frontend design, backend logic, growth limits, and features it is missing — and which of them are worth a survey of their own? | Open; a register rather than a survey — **twenty verified gaps**, six candidates dropped for lack of evidence and six refuted as documented decisions, ranked by the cost of leaving them and discounted by confidence. The recommendation is **three surveys, eight issues and five refusals by name**, and the first survey is reachability: nine of the twenty rows are one sentence — the app cannot find what it has already done — and the repository already contains the fix twice, at `/api/branches` and at `/api/knowledge/search`, which is a real parameterised search pointed at the operator's vault rather than at their own runs. Second is the landing boundary, which carries the register's only row whose failure lands in the operator's product: `landRun` merges without building or testing anything (`src/lib/land.ts:947-1057`), the setting that sounds like it does has one reader and it is the conflict assist (`:1275`), the concurrency guard covers one exported door of five, and no push or pull request exists anywhere in `src/`. **Nothing on the register violates a documented invariant** — every row is something `docs/agent/` never had an opinion about, and six candidates died because it did. Unverified: every reading of run history, because `DATA_DIR` is unreadable from a work cycle and the in-checkout database is stale — `SELECT COUNT(*) FROM runs` is the one-line falsifier that would reorder the top two; and whether any landing collision has ever occurred, which needs a running container |
| [OperatorInterface](OperatorInterface/README.md) | Sixteen pages and roughly fifty components are read by one operator supervising money-spending agents. Where does this interface fail a reader who is not the person who built it, and what is worth changing? | Open; one recommendation, and it is **six hex values, a dozen class strings and one test file**: repair four measured contrast failures and then pin them, in that order, because a floor asserted over today's tokens pins the failures instead. The four: the focus ring is `--accent` at 45% alpha and measures 1.93:1 to 2.26:1 against its own surround on every focusable element (1.4.11 asks 3:1; 75% is the first round figure that clears it everywhere); `--fg-faint` is 3.19:1 to 3.62:1 on every light surface at 11 to 13px, and reaches 192 rendered strings through `ListRow`'s description, `Hint` and `Empty`, which is where `docs/agent/ui-density-audit.md` deliberately *moved* facts a reader needs; white on `--danger` is 2.82:1 in dark on every irreversible button, where `globals.css:76-80` documents inventing `--tint`/`--tint-fg` to solve the same failure for blue and measures 5.06:1 for it; and `--fg-muted` is 3.54:1 on the bezel, which is the `/settings` section chips. `--fg-faint` has **no** repair as a token (the lightest value clearing 4.5:1 on all four light surfaces is `#6d6d71`, against `--fg-muted`'s `#68686d`), so the palette holds two text weights at AA in light mode, not three, and the fix is at three kit call sites. **Refused by name:** axe-core in CI (jsdom cannot resolve `light-dark()` inside `color-mix()`, so it reports `incomplete` on all four findings, and the affordable configuration is a rendering harness wearing an accessibility argument), raising the type scale (`globals.css:707-711` already refused it in writing, and browser zoom is a better instrument for an angular criterion than a token), and a progressive-disclosure restructure of `/settings` (two named prohibitions in the density audit, plus SearchPilot 2020 at +12% and Peytchev et al. 2010 at 36-45% to 60.7% both pointing the other way; the real gap is a field search, which is GapRegister F6). The keyboard pass **dissolves**: roving tabindex, `tabIndex={0}` on scroll containers, a native `<dialog>`, and the only thing wrong with it is a colour. Unverified: **no browser was driven at any viewport and no screen reader was used**, so nothing here is a judgement about how anything looks; the falsifier is one reading of the ring at 75%, which turns a halo into a nearly solid outline in a palette explicitly imitating AppKit; and the document-outline finding (eleven peer `<h2>`s on `/`, three of which are the cost-source containers) is filed as a question rather than a change because the instrument that would settle it is the one nobody here has |
| [ContinuousImprovement](ContinuousImprovement/README.md) | How does an install stop its runs re-deriving what earlier runs already established — the same mistakes and the same reading — and what does each way of doing it cost? | Open; one recommendation, and it is **against building a memory** — build the cross-run readout and the contention card, ship the prior-read pointer behind a 50% holdout in the same change, and refuse five of the eleven options by name. 73.2% of `Read` calls are of a path an earlier run on the same repository already read, but 50.6% of those are files the run then edits, so the addressable share is 36.1%; the repeated-*mistake* corpus is two ending-level failures in 294 runs. The survey's own control measured a generated in-prompt notice reproducing the exact command it names in 56 of 66 runs against 7 of a matched control of 175 — a 21x lift on the command form against only 1.1x on the behaviour — where CLAUDE.md's gate, in the same message, is declined by 101 of 112, so what is complied with is specificity rather than position. Unverified: `d`, the share of aimed-at reading a pointer actually removes rather than reorders, which nothing in this repository measures and which every saving claim multiplies by; and whether the gate decline is about position or content, which is the fortnight-long holdout that decides the runner-up |
